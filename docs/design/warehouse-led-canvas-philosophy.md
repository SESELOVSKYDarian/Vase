# Signal Cartography

Signal Cartography treats light as a mapped material: each point is both a physical coordinate and a quiet promise of direction. Space is organized as a disciplined field of repeated marks, with enough negative space for every state to remain legible. The grid feels engineered rather than decorative, meticulously calibrated so a glance reveals availability, occupation, and current intent.

Color carries the information. Deep graphite establishes the field; muted mineral greens describe dormant circuitry; precise emerald identifies the active selection; restrained amber marks territory already assigned. The palette is deliberately narrow and painstakingly tuned so status is communicated before any label is read, with master-level contrast in both light and dark environments.

Scale and rhythm emerge from one hundred equal nodes. Repetition creates calm, while four selected lights form a recognizable micro-composition inside the larger system. Each circle, gap, baseline, and edge is placed with the care of an instrument panel refined through countless iterations. The visual must feel tactile without imitating hardware literally.

Composition follows operational hierarchy: the strip dominates, sparse numeric anchors orient the eye, and compact counters confirm the action. Text remains minimal and clinical, never explanatory when shape and color can carry meaning. The result should look painstakingly crafted by someone at the top of their field, where precision itself becomes the aesthetic.

Interaction is expressed through visual certainty. Free, occupied, hovered, and selected states must never be ambiguous or depend on color alone; outlines, luminosity, and weight reinforce the chromatic language. Every transition should preserve spatial stability, making the final artifact feel like a deeply considered map of an invisible electrical system.
